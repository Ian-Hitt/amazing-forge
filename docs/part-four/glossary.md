# Glossary

Every term the game uses, in one place. Definitions are short on purpose — this is a place to
jog your memory mid-game, not to learn the rules from scratch.

**Action move.** A move that resolves a hero's attempt — you roll 2d6, add your modifier, and read
the result as a Strong Hit, Weak Hit, or Miss. The dice decide whether you pulled it off.

**Antagonist.** The force working against the heroes. Usually a person (a villain), but it can just
as easily be a thing — a desert, a plague, a ticking storm. Always call it the antagonist so you
remember it doesn't have to be a "bad guy" with a face.

**Antagonist Track.** The antagonist's clock: 5 boxes in an Episode, 9 in a Movie. It advances one
box three ways — a Devil's Bargain, a Miss showing doubles, or a hero going Out of Action. Its odd
boxes are Bad Guys Close In beats; filling the last box means the antagonist wins the Story.

**Ask the Dice.** The quick 1d6 yes/no oracle for settling an open question at the table.

**Ask the Oracle.** The move for when you're stuck on *what happens next* — roll on the Story Spark
table for an idea to build on.

**Asset.** One of your three specific "amazing at" things — a signature talent, a favorite tool, a
loyal friend. You argue that an Asset fits the moment (the one-sentence test); when it does, you get
+1.

**Bad Guys Close In.** The narrated antagonist beat you play out whenever an odd box of the
Antagonist Track fills. Cut away and show the antagonist gaining ground. (On a printed Story Arc
sheet it's abbreviated "Closing In.")

**Boon.** A signature move you buy onto an Asset card with Growth. Boons make you more *versatile* —
never bigger numbers.

**Broken Asset.** An Asset knocked offline when a hero goes Out of Action. It gives no +1 until
Downtime restores it. It's temporary, and never a penalty.

**Challenge.** An active, multi-roll obstacle with its own track — a fight, a chase, a locked vault.

**Challenge Roll.** A 2d6 roll made during a Challenge that marks progress on the Challenge Track.

**Challenge Track.** The row of boxes you fill to overcome a Challenge — 3 boxes (Normal), 6 (Hard),
or 9 (Epic).

**Climax.** The final, usually Epic, Challenge of a Story. Completing its last Milestone simply *is*
the win — there's no separate finishing roll.

**Co-op play.** Playing cooperatively as a group with no Guide — the table shares the Guide's job.

**Concept.** The "movie poster" pitch for your character in a line or two.

**Devil's Bargain.** On a Miss, you may refuse the Readiness loss *and* upgrade the roll to a Strong
Hit — in exchange for advancing the Antagonist Track one box. Always your choice. Never allowed on a
roll that would knock you Out of Action.

**Doubles.** The crit rule. When your 2d6 come up matching: on a Hit, bump it up one tier; on a Miss,
the antagonist advances one box instead.

**Downtime.** The reset between Stories. Its job is restoring Broken Assets (and handling Growth
bookkeeping). It is *not* a healing move — Recovery Scenes already heal, for free, any time.

**Episode.** A complete short Story — 3 Milestones and a 5-box Antagonist Track — told in about one
sitting.

**Fall back.** Retreating from a losing Challenge to regroup. You lose the Challenge's progress but
heal fully, for free (it's handled as a Recovery Scene).

**Frame move.** A move that shifts the fiction in the moment with no track. When it rolls, the dice
suggest a direction rather than judge a hero, so it can't be "failed."

**Genre.** The mood of your story, set in Worldbuilding. One of seven: Adventure, Mystery, Horror,
Sci-Fi, Caper, Drama, Post-Apocalypse.

**Genre Kit.** A per-genre toolkit — the feel, worldbuilding prompts, archetypes, and a d100 Asset
table — one for each of the seven genres.

**Growth.** The advancement currency. You earn 1 every third Milestone and spend it on Boons or new
Assets. (Never "XP.")

**Growth Track.** Your personal row of boxes tracking earned Growth.

**Guide.** The person running the game — part director, part oracle. (Never "GM.")

**Guided play.** Playing with a Guide.

**Hero / character.** Your in-fiction persona. "Hero" and "character" mean the same thing here.

**Hero Track.** A Story's progress track — one box per Milestone. Fill the last box (finish the last
Milestone) and you win the Story.

**Lights, Camera, Action.** The single d6 oracle roll made when a Story begins, setting how it opens:
**5–6** a clear purpose, **3–4** a general idea, **1–2** trouble finds you first.

**Mend.** A small, risky patch you can attempt in any scene (in a Challenge it's your turn instead of
a Challenge Roll): heal a little on yourself or an ally — Strong +3 / Weak +2 / **Miss −1** Readiness,
capped at 9. No usage limit, never advances the antagonist, can't revive someone Out of Action.

**Milestone.** A chapter of the story — a cluster of Scenes. Each one checks a Hero Track box, and
every third Milestone earns each hero 1 Growth. Roughly an hour of play.

**Miss.** A roll of 6 or less — the worst of the three outcomes. See Pay the Price.

**Move.** Any defined procedure you invoke at the table. Every move is an Action, Progress, or Frame
move.

**Movie.** A complete feature-length Story — 6 Milestones and a 9-box Antagonist Track — told over
about two or three sessions.

**Out of Action.** What happens when Readiness hits 0: the hero sits out the rest of the Scene
(knocked out, never killed), breaks an Asset, and advances the Antagonist Track one box. A Recovery
Scene brings them back.

**Outstanding Success.** Doubles on a Strong Hit — a spectacular result that also grants **+1 to your
next roll**, and during a Challenge fills **two** boxes instead of one.

**Pay the Price.** The complication that follows a Weak Hit or a Miss — the story cost of not fully
succeeding.

**Player.** A person at the table controlling a hero. (A player is not the same as a character.)

**Progress.** What you mark on a track — Hero, Antagonist, Challenge, or Growth.

**Progress move.** A move that opens or closes a track (a Story or a Challenge).

**Quit the Story Arc.** Giving up a Story entirely — the terminal fall back. The antagonist wins, and
a lost Story seeds the next one.

**Readiness.** Your hero's single Health Bar — body, mind, and supplies in one 0–9 track. Starts and
maxes at 9. Weak Hit costs −1, a Miss −2. At 0 you go Out of Action. Readiness is a pacing signal —
when to fall back — not a life meter; heroes can't die.

**Recovery Scene.** The fall-back-and-regroup move: the whole party heals fully back to 9, for free —
no roll, and it never advances the antagonist. Fall back as often as the fiction allows.

**Regular Roll.** A one-off 2d6 roll with no track behind it.

**Scene.** A single beat of play — a roll or two, or a whole Challenge. Milestones are built from
Scenes.

**Season.** A collection of Stories sharing a throughline. It's a way of stringing Stories together,
not a story size with its own boxes.

**Series.** The whole game — a run of Seasons. Also a collection pattern, never a box size.

**Stat.** One of your two broad "naturally good at" areas, chosen from a closed five — **Strong**,
**Quick**, **Clever**, **Sneaky**, **Charming**. Which Stat an action calls for is decided
objectively by the fiction; when it's one of your two, you get +1.

**Story / Story Arc.** One spine — a single central dramatic question — tracked with a Hero Track and
an Antagonist Track. Sized as an Episode or a Movie.

**Story Spark.** The small d6 idea table inside Ask the Oracle.

**Strong Hit / Weak Hit / Miss.** The three roll outcomes: **10+** a Strong Hit (you nail it), **7–9**
a Weak Hit (you do it, but Pay the Price), **6−** a Miss (it goes wrong).

**tonal dial.** The playful↔serious scale every genre can run on. Set it per table.

**Trade In.** Retiring one Asset to make room for a new one once you've hit the six-Asset ceiling.
