# Making a Location

*A quick generator for the places you didn't plan. Roll or pick — and walk in with a hook already waiting.*

Sooner or later the heroes go somewhere you hadn't thought about. They take the side road, kick down the wrong door, ask "what's in *there*?" You don't need a map or a plan — you need a place that's vivid enough to picture and already has a *reason to matter.* This builds one in three quick draws.

It works just as well for prep: roll a few up before a session and you've got a pocketful of places to drop in when the story wanders. And it pairs with everything else — lean on **Ask the Oracle** ([Chapter 11](../part-two/11-ask-the-oracle.md)) for any follow-up question, and let your **Genre Kit** ([Chapter 15](../part-three/15-genres.md)) color the result (a refuge in Horror is not a refuge in Caper).

## Build it in three draws

Roll once on each table (or just pick the one that sparks). The first two make the place *yours*; the third is the one that turns a backdrop into a scene.

**1. What kind of place is it?** *(d10)*

| d10 | The place |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A threshold** — a gate, a bridge, a border, a doorway between one world and the next. |
| 2 | **A place of trade** — a market, a shop, a hub where goods and rumors change hands. |
| 3 | **A seat of power** — a hall, a throne room, an office; where the decisions get made. |
| 4 | **A refuge** — an inn, a haven, a safe-house; somewhere people catch their breath. |
| 5 | **A ruin** — abandoned, broken, reclaimed; a place that used to be something. |
| 6 | **A wild place** — forest, waste, sea, mountain; nature, indifferent or hungry. |
| 7 | **A place of secrets** — an archive, a temple, a vault, a lab; knowledge kept or hidden. |
| 8 | **A dwelling** — a home, a camp, a den; where someone lives, and what that reveals. |
| 9 | **A way through** — a road, a tunnel, a dock, a transit line; movement and passers-by. |
| 10 | **A hidden place** — a back room, an underlevel, somewhere you weren't meant to find. |

**2. What's the one striking detail?** *(d10)* — the thing players will remember it by.

| d10 | The detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | It's the wrong size — far bigger or smaller inside than it has any right to be. |
| 2 | A sound defines it — a constant drip, distant music, an unsettling hum, total silence. |
| 3 | A smell hits you first — woodsmoke, rot, perfume, ozone, the sea. |
| 4 | The light is strange — too bright, too gloomy, colored, flickering, no clear source. |
| 5 | It's covered in one thing — vines, dust, ice, graffiti, water, scratch-marks. |
| 6 | One object is here that absolutely does not belong. |
| 7 | It's mid-something — half-built, half-ruined, just abandoned, set up for an event. |
| 8 | It remembers a person — a shrine, a portrait, belongings, a name carved everywhere. |
| 9 | A single bold feature dominates, and the locals pointedly never mention it. |
| 10 | Roll twice and combine. |

**3. What's wrong here?** *(d10)* — the hook. Every place worth a scene has one.

| d10 | The trouble |
|---|---|
| 1 | Someone here wants something from the heroes — a favor, a toll, the truth. |
| 2 | It isn't as safe as it looks — a trap, a collapse, a tide, a patrol on its way. |
| 3 | Two groups here are about to clash, and the heroes just walked into the middle. |
| 4 | Something's missing — looted, vanished, or taken just before the heroes arrived. |
| 5 | A clue or a prize is here, but reaching it means crossing a line. |
| 6 | Someone is watching, and the heroes don't know it yet. |
| 7 | The way out just got harder than the way in. |
| 8 | The place is failing — flooding, burning, freezing, fading — on a clock. |
| 9 | The people here are frightened, and they think the heroes caused it (or can fix it). |
| 10 | What the heroes came for is here — but so is the antagonist's hand. *(Tick the Antagonist Track if it lands — [Chapter 10](../part-two/10-quests-and-the-antagonist-track.md).)* |

**And who's here?** *(d6, optional)* — if it matters, find out who shares the place.

| d6 | Who |
|---|---|
| 1 | One person, alone, with a reason to be. |
| 2 | A small group with a shared purpose. |
| 3 | A crowd — easy to hide in, hard to read. |
| 4 | Empty — but fresh signs someone just left. |
| 5 | Someone who very much shouldn't be here. |
| 6 | Roll on **[Making an NPC](npc-generator.md)** for whoever matters most. |

## Put it together

Three quick rolls — *a refuge · defined by a low constant hum · someone is watching* — and you have this:

> A roadside inn that hums faintly, like a struck wire you can feel in your teeth. It *should* be the safe stop the heroes needed — but a quiet traveler by the fire hasn't taken her eyes off the door since they walked in.

You didn't plan a single word of that, and it already has a mood, a memorable hook, and a scene waiting to happen. Ask the Oracle who the traveler is watching *for*, and you're off.

> **One trap to skip:** don't roll the whole world. You need the *one* place in front of the heroes, with its *one* problem. The next place is a new roll when they get there — not something to prepare three rooms deep.
