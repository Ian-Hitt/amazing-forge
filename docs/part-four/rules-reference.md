# The Whole Game — Rules Reference

*Every rule, no examples and no flavor — the complete mechanics, distilled. (Parts One–Three teach all of this in depth, with examples.)*

**What this is.** A **genre-agnostic, zero-prep storytelling game** — the same rules tell *any* kind of story (fantasy, sci-fi, mystery, horror, heist, drama, and on). Nobody writes the adventure ahead of time: you build a world, play the heroes, and **make the story up together as you go**, either with a **Guide** or fully **Co-op** (no Guide at all). The mechanics below are the tools that turn that improvised story into cinematic drama — "prep" *is* play.

---

## The Core Roll

Use this whenever your hero tries something risky and you want to find out how it goes. It's the one move everything else is built from.

- Roll **2d6 + 2** if one of your Assets fits what you're attempting, **+ 1** if none does.
- **10+** Strong Hit — clean success. · **7–9** Weak Hit — success, but **Pay the Price.** · **6−** Miss — it fails, and **Pay the Price.**
- **Doubles = Oracle's Blessing:** bump the result up one tier (Miss → Weak, Weak → Strong).
- On a Hit, **you narrate** what happens.
- A **Regular Roll** settles a moment in one roll (no track). A **Challenge** plays a big scene out on a track.
- **Aid Your Ally:** spend your roll to help a teammate — **Strong** they get +2 · **Weak** they get +1, you −1 · **Miss** nothing, you −2.

## Heroes

Each player makes one hero in about two minutes: a Concept, four Assets, and Readiness.

- **Concept** — a one-line "movie poster" pitch (*an Ice-Dragon Knight*, *a Grumpy Goblin Alchemist*).
- **Four Assets** — by default, picked by answering four questions (you don't have to, but most heroes are built this way):
    - ① **What are you best at?** Your **Attribute** — one broad pick from **Strong / Quick / Clever / Sneaky / Charming** (max one per hero).
    - ② **What did you train in?** A **skill.**
    - ③ **What's your signature item or companion?**
    - ④ **One Wild pick** of your own. *(Swap among ②–④ freely if your concept wants.)*
- An Asset that **fits a roll gives +2**. A good Asset is broad enough to come up often, specific enough that it clearly doesn't cover everything (the Attribute is the one deliberately broad pick).
- **Readiness** starts at **9** — body, mind, and supplies in one track.

## Readiness, Pay the Price & Out of Action

Readiness is your hero's single resource. It falls when things go wrong, and bottoming out sidelines you — but never kills you.

- **Pay the Price** on a Weak Hit (**−1**) or Miss (**−2**) — always, plus the most fitting in-fiction complication.
- **Readiness max** starts at **9**, drops **1 each Recovery Scene** (floor **4**), and resets to 9 at Downtime. You recover only up to your **current max.**
- **Out of Action** at 0 Readiness: you can't act, and **one Asset breaks** (gives no +2 until restored at Downtime). **Heroes can't die.**

## Recovery (three ways)

How Readiness comes back: a quick risky patch in the moment, a full regroup that hands the villain ground, and a clean slate between Stories.

- **Mend** — *any scene, on your turn:* patch yourself or an ally. **Strong +3 / Weak +2 / Miss −1.** No usage cap; **never advances the villain;** can't revive an Out-of-Action hero; heals only to current max.
- **Recovery Scene** — *fall back & regroup at a safe lull:* the whole party recovers **up to current max**, the **Antagonist Track surges one box**, and your **max drops 1.** The **only** thing that advances the villain.
- **Downtime** — *between Story Arcs:* reset max to **9**, heal everyone to full, restore Broken Assets. No surge.

## Challenges

When a moment is a real set-piece — a fight, a chase, a daring escape — play it out as a Challenge: a scene resolved over a row of boxes instead of a single roll.

- A track of boxes. **Easy = 1 box per player** (Medium = +1, Hard = +2).
- Take turns (everyone acts before anyone repeats). Each roll: **Strong** fill a box · **Weak** fill a box + Pay the Price · **Miss** no box + Pay the Price.
- Fill the track = the obstacle is overcome. Enemies have **no stats** — the track *is* the enemy.
- **Fall back** to bail out: lose the track's progress and take a **Recovery Scene** (heal up, villain surges).
- **Showdown** *(the finishing move for the last box):* one charged roll — **Strong** win + a bonus · **Weak** win + Pay the Price · **Miss** add a box and escalate (never an outright loss; never advances the villain).

## Story Arcs & the Antagonist Track

A Story Arc is the shape of a whole adventure: a race between the heroes' progress and the villain's. Here's how to build one, run the villain's clock, and win or lose it.

- **Set it up:** name the **Goal** (a one-sentence victory condition), a few **Milestones** (concrete, pointable steps — you'll know each the moment it's done), and an **antagonist** (a person, faction, or *force* — a desert, a plague, a deadline; give it a face and a goal). Then draw the two tracks.
- A **Story Arc** is one spine: a **Story Arc Track** (your progress — one box per **Milestone**) and a short **Antagonist Track** beneath it whose **top box is the reserved climax.**
- **Sizes (the only two with boxes):** **Episode** = 3 Milestones / **2-box** Antagonist Track (one sitting). **Movie** = 6 Milestones / **3-box** (a ~2-hour feature over 2–3 sessions). **Season** and **Series** are *collections* of Story Arcs, not sizes — you run long by **chaining** Stories.
- A **Milestone** ≈ 40 minutes of play (a few Scenes, 1–2 of them Challenges); mark a box on significant headway.
- The **Antagonist Track advances only when the heroes take a Recovery Scene** — the **Surge** (play out the villain gaining ground). Its **top box** (the climax) fills only at the **Showdown** — or early if an Out-of-Action hero forces a Recovery Scene into it, which **loses the Story before the finale.**
- **Win:** fill the Story Arc Track and win the Showdown, then take Downtime. **Lose:** the Antagonist Track fills, or you **Quit the Story Arc** (the terminal fall-back — no penalty; the loss seeds the next Story).
- **B-plots & character arcs are threads:** a light progress track with **no Antagonist Track / no lose-clock**; they resolve into the spine's climax.

## Opening Rolls (generative — you can't fail them)

One roll that sets how a new Story Arc, or a Challenge you choose to start, *opens* — it shapes the temperature of the scene, never whether you succeed.

- **Start a Story Arc** (2d6 + 1): sets how the opening feels — **10+** clear purpose · **7–9** one complication already in play · **6−** trouble finds you first.
- **Start a Challenge** (2d6 + 1, only when *you* choose to open one): a one-time **±1 to the first roll** — **10+** +1 · **7–9** 0 · **6−** −1.

## Ask the Oracle (when nobody knows what's next)

Use this whenever the story stalls and the table isn't sure what happens next — especially in Co-op, where it's the engine that generates the story.

- Name the next **Milestone**; do the **obvious** thing; or hand it to the dice:
- **Story Spark (d6):** 1 new face · 2 discovery · 3 the threat closes in · 4 a door opens · 5 a cost surfaces · 6 a hard choice.
- **Ask the Dice (1d6):** **1** no, and worse · **2–3** no · **4–5** yes · **6** yes, and better. (+1 if a yes is likely, −1 if unlikely.)

## Growth (advancement — horizontal only)

How heroes grow over a campaign — earning new tricks and options, never bigger numbers, so the math never inflates.

- Earn **1 Growth** every **3rd Milestone** the party marks (on any Story Arc; cumulative, never resets).
- **Boon — 2 Growth:** a signature move attached to an Asset (max 2 per Asset).
- **New Asset — 5 Growth:** ceiling of 6 Assets; at six, **Trade In** (retire one) to add another.

## Running the Game

How a session actually runs — with a Guide or fully Co-op — and the one habit that keeps it cinematic.

- **Guided** (one Guide runs the world) or **Co-op** (no Guide — Ask the Oracle is the engine).
- **The Golden Rule:** when unsure, do the obvious or coolest next thing. Cut to the exciting part — no dice when there's no real risk, no boring scenes.
- **Setup:** build a world (ten quick questions — Ch 14), make heroes, set up the first Story Arc, and open in the middle of the action.
