# Chapter 1 — Build Your World

Every adventure needs a world to happen in. Before you make heroes or start your Story Arc, your group spends a few minutes inventing the place your story lives. This is **Worldbuilding**, and it's the first thing you do together.

Here's the good news: you can build a whole world in about fifteen minutes by answering ten quick questions out loud as a group — and if the table's having fun with it, there's no harm in taking longer. There are no wrong answers. The goal isn't a finished, detailed setting — it's just enough of a shared picture that everyone's imagining the same kind of place. The rest fills itself in as you play.

**How to do it:** read each question aloud and talk it over. Someone tosses out an idea, others build on it, and you keep whatever the table likes. Some questions will spark a big conversation; others you'll answer in five seconds or skip entirely. Both are fine. Jot down anything you want to remember.

### The Ten Questions

**1. The Genre — What's the mood of our story?**
Adventure, Mystery, Horror, Sci-Fi, Caper, Drama, or Post-Apocalypse? Pick the feeling you want — and how playful or serious it should be. *(Example: "Spooky, but the fun kind of spooky.")* *(Part Four has a full kit for each genre.)*

**2. The Setting — Where and when does it happen?**
A medieval kingdom, deep space, an underwater city, the present day with a strange twist? *(Example: "A floating city above the clouds.")*

**3. The Denizens — Who lives here?**
The kinds of people, creatures, or beings we'll meet — and might play as. *(Example: "Humans, talking animals, and clockwork people.")*

**4. Magic — How does it work, if at all?**
Common or rare? Cast with wands, granted by spirits, pulled from inside you? Or no magic at all? *(Example: "Rare, and only a few people can do it.")*

**5. Technology — What level are we at?**
Swords and shields? Steam-powered gears? Laser blasters and robots? Lost technology nobody understands anymore? *(Example: "Mostly old-fashioned, but with a few strange machines left behind.")*

**6. The Leadership — Who's in charge?**
A king or queen, a powerful company, a council, or nobody at all? *(Example: "A council of elders who rule the city.")*

**7. The Enforcement — How do they stay in charge?**
An army, spies, robot guards, or simply the people's respect? *(Example: "Sky-patrol guards on flying machines.")*

**8. The Threats — What are the two or three biggest dangers right now?**
An ancient evil waking up, a shortage of something vital, a rival nation, a spreading monster? *(Example: "The clouds holding the city up are slowly thinning.")*

**9. The Forbidden — What's one place, object, or action that's off-limits?**
Forbidden ruins, a kind of magic no one's allowed to use, the land beyond the great wall? *(Example: "Nobody is allowed below the cloud line.")*

**10. The Reputation — When people see a group like yours, what do they think?**
Brave protectors, troublemakers, legends, total unknowns? *(Example: "Reckless kids who don't know their place.")*

### You're Done — That's a World

Answer those ten questions and you've built a setting rich enough to adventure in. Notice how the answers already hint at stories: a floating city that's slowly sinking, a forbidden world below the clouds, and a group nobody takes seriously. You haven't planned an adventure — but you can already feel where the trouble is.

Don't worry about filling every gap now. If a question didn't grab anyone, leave it loose; you'll discover the answer when it comes up in play. The world only needs to be as detailed as your story so far.
