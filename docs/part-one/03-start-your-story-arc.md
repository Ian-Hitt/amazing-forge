# Chapter 3 — Start Your Story Arc

You've got a world and you've got heroes. The last thing you need before you start playing is something worth doing — a **Story Arc.** This is the big goal your heroes are chasing, the thing the whole adventure is about. Setting one up takes just a couple of minutes, and then you're playing.

### Name Your Goal and Milestones

A Story Arc has two parts: the **Goal** and the **Milestones.**

**The Goal** is your finish line — the one clear thing that means the heroes have won. Say it in a single sentence, big and exciting:

- "Recover the stolen Sun Crystal."
- "Escape the sinking sky-city before it falls."
- "Find out who's been haunting the old lighthouse."

**The Milestones** are the steps along the way — each a mini-goal, a real achievement that gets the heroes closer to the finish. A first-game adventure has room for **three:** enough to feel like a real adventure, short enough to finish in a single sitting. You can name all three now, or just name the **first** and play to find out what the rest turn out to be — either works.

Say your Goal is "Recover the stolen Sun Crystal." Your three Milestones might be:

1. **Find the secret entrance** to the goblins' mountain.
2. **Cross the Bottomless Chasm** that splits the cave in two.
3. **Get the Crystal back** from the Goblin King and escape.

Notice each Milestone is a clear win you can point at — not a vague "keep going." You don't need to plan *how* the heroes will pull each one off. That's the fun part you discover by playing.

### Name What Stands in Your Way

Every good story needs something pushing back. Before you start, decide on your **antagonist** — the main force trying to stop the heroes from reaching their Goal. Knowing two things is enough: *what do we want*, and *who's going to make it hard?*

An antagonist is often a person — the Goblin King, a rival treasure-hunter, a haunting ghost. But it doesn't have to be a person at all. The thing standing in your way can be almost anything:

- On a long trek across a frozen wasteland, the antagonist might be **the weather and the land itself.**
- In a race against time, it might be **the ticking clock** before the volcano erupts.
- In a mystery, it might be **the secret** that someone is desperately trying to keep buried.

Just name it out loud so everyone knows what they're up against. Your antagonist gets its own track too (you'll draw it in a moment) — the bad guys are racing you to the finish.

### Draw the Two Tracks

Now make the race visible. On a scrap of paper, draw a row of **three empty boxes** — one for each Milestone. This is your **Hero Track:**

```
HERO TRACK:         [ ]  [ ]  [ ]
```

Every time the heroes complete a Milestone, check off one box. When all three are filled, the heroes reach the Goal and **win the Story Arc.**

Then draw a second, longer row right underneath — **five boxes** — for the **Antagonist Track:**

```
ANTAGONIST TRACK:   [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]  [ ]
```

This is the bad guys' progress toward *their* goal. It fills one box at a time as the antagonist gains ground (you'll learn exactly how in Chapter 5) — and if it fills all the way before the heroes reach their finish, the bad guys win and the Story Arc is lost. The two rows side by side are the whole story in miniature: a race between the heroes and the antagonist.

A quick way to picture a Milestone: it's a *chapter* of your movie, made up of a few **scenes.** Some scenes are over in a single dice roll ("you talk your way past the guard"); others are big enough to play out in detail. Chapters 4 and 5 show you how scenes work — for now, just know each Milestone is a little stretch of story, not one single moment.

> *Part Two has more for Stories — the bigger **Movie** size, chaining Stories into a Season or a whole Series, the full Antagonist Track and the ways the bad guys gain ground, a quick "Lights, Camera, Action" roll that sets how each new adventure opens, and how the scenes inside a Milestone range from a single roll to a full set-piece. For your first game, keep it to one Story Arc, three Milestones, and the two tracks — and jump straight into the action, as the next section shows.*

### Jump In: In Media Res

Here's the most important rule for starting strong: **don't begin with the boring setup. Begin in the middle of the action.**

Filmmakers call this *in media res* — Latin for "into the middle of things." A movie about slaying a dragon doesn't open with the heroes shopping for rope and chatting in a tavern. It opens at the mouth of the smoking cave, or with the heroes already sprinting from the burning village. Start your game the same way.

So skip ahead to the first exciting moment. If the first Milestone is "find the secret entrance," don't start the group packing their bags at home — start them halfway up the freezing mountain with a snowstorm closing in and a hidden door somewhere on the cliff face.

Whoever's setting the scene — the Guide, or the group together in Co-op play — paints that opening picture in a sentence or two. Then comes the question that kicks off everything:

> *"What do you do?"*

And that's it. The moment someone answers, you're playing. The next chapter shows you what happens when a hero tries something risky — when it's time to pick up the dice.
