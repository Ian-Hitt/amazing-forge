# Chapter 17 — Making Locations

*A quick generator for the places you didn't plan. Roll or pick — and walk in with a hook already waiting.*

Sooner or later the heroes go somewhere you hadn't thought about. They take the side road, kick down the wrong door, ask "what's in *there*?" You don't need a map or a plan — you need a place that's vivid enough to picture and already has a *reason to matter.*

Here's the idea that makes this work: **a location has a *function*, the same way an NPC has a *role*.** Just as you generate a person by asking "why are they in the story?", you generate a place by asking "why does the story come *here* — what do the heroes get?" A seedy quarter is where you buy what you can't buy legally; a seat of power is where you go to petition or confront. Name the function and the place earns its scene.

Every place type below is written to be **portable** — interpret it for your world. A "seat of power" is a throne room, a corporate boardroom, or a warlord's tent; lean on **Ask the Oracle** ([Chapter 11](../part-two/11-ask-the-oracle.md)) for any follow-up, and let your **Genre Kit** ([Chapter 15](15-genres.md)) set the dressing (a refuge in Horror is not a refuge in Caper). It works for prep, too — roll a few up before a session and drop them in when the story wanders.

## Build it in three draws

The first table is the heart — it gives you *what the place is* and *why the heroes are here.* The other two make it vivid and turn it into a scene.

**1. What's the place — and what's its function?** *(d20)*

| d20 | The place — *why the heroes come* |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The Seedy Quarter** — back-alleys and bad reputations. *To get what you can't get legally: information, contraband, a dangerous ally — for a price.* |
| 2 | **The Crossroads Market** — stalls, crowds, a hundred conversations. *To buy, sell, barter, and overhear; everyone and everything passes through.* |
| 3 | **The Seat of Power** — the hall, the court, the headquarters. *To petition, infiltrate, or confront whoever's in charge.* |
| 4 | **The Watch-Post** — gate, checkpoint, guardhouse, border. *To get past the people whose whole job is stopping you.* |
| 5 | **The Refuge** — inn, safehouse, haven. *To rest, regroup, hide, and hear who else is laying low.* |
| 6 | **The House of Knowing** — archive, library, temple, lab. *To learn something true that no one will simply tell you.* |
| 7 | **The Ruin** — a dead place, abandoned and reclaimed. *To recover what was lost here — and disturb what's moved in.* |
| 8 | **The Wild Expanse** — forest, waste, sea, mountain, open dark. *To cross it, survive it, or find the one thing hidden out in it.* |
| 9 | **The Underbelly** — sewers, tunnels, undercity, the level below. *To move unseen, or reach what the surface walled off.* |
| 10 | **The Stronghold** — fortress, vault, prison, compound. *To break in, break out, or take something well-defended.* |
| 11 | **The Workplace** — mine, farm, dock, factory, forge. *Resources at the source — and the workers, bosses, and accidents around them.* |
| 12 | **The Den of Vice** — tavern, gambling hall, fight-pit, lounge. *Where tongues loosen, debts are struck, and the wrong people relax.* |
| 13 | **The Gathering** — festival, market-day, funeral, council, wedding. *Everyone who matters is in one room — and so is the trouble.* |
| 14 | **The Threshold** — bridge, gate, portal, port of departure. *The crossing between one world and the next; pass through, or be stopped.* |
| 15 | **The Sanctum** — shrine, healer's, elder's home, holy ground. *For aid, mending, blessing, or counsel — and the strings attached.* |
| 16 | **The Hideout** — a gang's lair, a rebel cell, a secret base. *To find the people working outside the law — as friend or as threat.* |
| 17 | **The Dwelling** — someone's home, room, camp, quarters. *To meet a person on their own ground, and read who they really are.* |
| 18 | **The Monument** — the landmark, the wonder, the great old thing. *It means something to everyone; witness it, control it, or unlock it.* |
| 19 | **The Wound** — disaster site, plague ward, battlefield, blight. *Something went terribly wrong here; the heroes walk into the aftermath.* |
| 20 | **The Edge of the Map** — frontier, forbidden zone, the place none return from. *Because the answer — or the danger — is where no sane person goes.* |

**2. What's the one striking detail?** *(d20)* — the thing players remember it by.

| d20 | The detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | It's the wrong size — far bigger or smaller inside than it should be. |
| 2 | A sound defines it — a constant drip, distant music, an unsettling hum, total silence. |
| 3 | A smell hits you first — woodsmoke, rot, perfume, ozone, the sea. |
| 4 | The light is strange — too bright, too gloomy, colored, flickering, no clear source. |
| 5 | It's covered in one thing — vines, dust, ice, graffiti, water, scratch-marks. |
| 6 | One object is here that absolutely does not belong. |
| 7 | It's mid-something — half-built, half-ruined, just abandoned, set up for an event. |
| 8 | It remembers a person — a shrine, a portrait, belongings, a name carved everywhere. |
| 9 | A single bold feature dominates, and the locals pointedly never mention it. |
| 10 | It's far too crowded — or eerily empty for what it is. |
| 11 | Everything's improvised — patched, scavenged, held together with hope. |
| 12 | It's beautiful in a way that feels wrong for this place. |
| 13 | The temperature's off — freezing, sweltering, or clammy when it shouldn't be. |
| 14 | It's clearly been fought over — fresh damage, barricades, old stains. |
| 15 | A barrier splits it — a wall, a curtain, a line nobody will cross. |
| 16 | It's far newer, or far older, than everything around it. |
| 17 | Animals or vermin have made it theirs. |
| 18 | A rule governs it — posted, spoken, or just obvious — and everyone obeys. |
| 19 | It's been dressed up to hide what it really is. |
| 20 | Roll twice and combine. |

**3. What's wrong here?** *(d20)* — the hook. Every place worth a scene has one.

| d20 | The trouble |
|---|---|
| 1 | Someone here wants something from the heroes — a favor, a toll, the truth. |
| 2 | It isn't as safe as it looks — a trap, a collapse, a tide, a patrol on its way. |
| 3 | Two groups here are about to clash, and the heroes just walked into the middle. |
| 4 | Something's missing — looted, vanished, or taken just before the heroes arrived. |
| 5 | A clue or a prize is here, but reaching it means crossing a line. |
| 6 | Someone is watching, and the heroes don't know it yet. |
| 7 | The way out just got harder than the way in. |
| 8 | The place is failing — flooding, burning, freezing, fading — on a clock. |
| 9 | The people here are frightened, and they blame the heroes (or beg them to fix it). |
| 10 | What the heroes came for is here — but so is the antagonist's hand. *(Tick the Antagonist Track if it lands — [Chapter 10](../part-two/10-quests-and-the-antagonist-track.md).)* |
| 11 | The person they need is here, but won't — or can't — talk freely. |
| 12 | There's a price of entry nobody mentioned until now. |
| 13 | A familiar face is here, on purpose, and it's no coincidence. |
| 14 | The rules of the place forbid exactly what the heroes need to do. |
| 15 | The help on offer comes with strings the heroes won't like. |
| 16 | The heroes are mistaken for someone — expected, wanted, or owed. |
| 17 | Time is short — the place, or someone in it, is leaving soon. |
| 18 | What looked like the goal is a decoy, a fake, or already gone. |
| 19 | Getting what they want will cost someone here who doesn't deserve it. |
| 20 | It's a trap, set *for* the heroes specifically. |

**And who's here?** *(d8, optional)* — if it matters, find out who shares the place.

| d8 | Who |
|---|---|
| 1 | One person, alone, with a reason to be. |
| 2 | A small group with a shared purpose. |
| 3 | A crowd — easy to hide in, hard to read. |
| 4 | Empty — but fresh signs someone just left. |
| 5 | Someone who very much shouldn't be here. |
| 6 | Whoever runs the place — and they've noticed you. |
| 7 | Two parties who don't know about each other yet. |
| 8 | Roll on **[Making an NPC](18-making-npcs.md)** for whoever matters most. |

## Put it together

*The Seedy Quarter (get what you can't get legally, for a price) · defined by distant, tinny music · someone is watching* gives you:

> A back-alley district where music drifts from somewhere you can't quite pin down, and everything's for sale if you know who to ask. The heroes came to buy a secret — but they can't shake the feeling someone's been watching them since they turned off the main road.

The function told you *why they're here* (and what they can get), the detail made it stick, and the trouble gave the scene its edge — none of it planned. Ask the Oracle who's watching, and you're playing.

> **One trap to skip:** don't roll the whole world. You need the *one* place in front of the heroes, with its *one* problem. The next place is a new roll when they get there — not three rooms prepared in advance.
