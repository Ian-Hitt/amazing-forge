# Chapter 18 — Making NPCs

*A quick generator for the people the heroes meet. You need two things, fast: why they're in the story, and one detail nobody forgets.*

Players talk to *people* — constantly. The guard at the gate, the trader with the rumor, the frightened kid who saw everything. Most of these you'll invent the second a player turns to them, and here's the good news: you don't need a name, a backstory, and a personality to play one well. You need to know **why this person is in the story** — their role — and **one vivid detail** so they stick in everyone's memory. That's a playable NPC in about five seconds. (Locations work the same way — a role for people, a *function* for places. See [Making Locations](17-making-locations.md).)

Lean on **Ask the Oracle** ([Chapter 11](../part-two/11-ask-the-oracle.md)) for anything you haven't decided — *are they lying? will they help?* — and let your **Genre Kit** ([Chapter 15](15-genres.md)) flavor them.

## Build one in two draws

**1. What's their role in the story?** *(d10)* — this is *why they exist*: the job they do for your scene. Decide this first; everything else hangs off it.

| d10 | The role |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The Helper** — on the heroes' side, for now. Offers aid, shelter, a hand — maybe at a price. |
| 2 | **The Obstacle** — in the way, just doing their job: a guard, a gatekeeper, a stubborn official. |
| 3 | **The Source** — knows something the heroes need: a witness, a gossip, an expert, an old-timer. |
| 4 | **The Petitioner** — wants *the heroes* to do something: brings a problem, a plea, a job. |
| 5 | **The Innocent** — the one in danger or in need; the reason to care, the stakes made personal. |
| 6 | **The Rival** — wants what the heroes want, and got there first (or is racing them for it). |
| 7 | **The Wildcard** — has their own agenda that cuts across everyone's; could help or wreck it all. |
| 8 | **The Authority** — holds power over the situation; the heroes need their say-so (a permission, a verdict, a blessing). Doesn't block by *doing* — blocks by *deciding.* |
| 9 | **The Connector** — the hub who knows everyone; their value isn't facts, it's introductions and access. The door to other people. |
| 10 | **The Hidden Hand** — seems harmless or friendly, but is quietly working against the heroes. *(Use sparingly — the betrayer, the spy, the thing wearing a face.)* |

**2. What's the one evocative detail?** *(d20)* — the hook that makes them a *person*, not a placeholder.

| d20 | The detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | A voice you won't forget — a rasp, a boom, a whisper, an odd lilt. |
| 2 | Never stops moving — fidgets, paces, taps, can't hold still. |
| 3 | One striking feature — a scar, mismatched eyes, impossible height, a missing finger. |
| 4 | Dressed wrong for the place — too fine, too shabby, out of season, out of era. |
| 5 | A constant companion — an animal, a tool, a weapon they handle like a pet. |
| 6 | A verbal tic — a catchphrase, a nervous laugh, repeats your name, never finishes sentences. |
| 7 | Too calm, or too intense — unnervingly serene, or wired and over-eager. |
| 8 | Marked by their work — stained hands, burns, calluses, a uniform half-worn. |
| 9 | Hiding something physically — won't show their hands, their face, what's in the bag. |
| 10 | Oddly familiar — looks or sounds like someone, and you can't quite place it. |
| 11 | A tell when they lie — and they're doing it right now. |
| 12 | Carries their own weather — always cold, always cheerful, always grieving. |
| 13 | Treats the heroes as if they're already friends — or already enemies. |
| 14 | Talks to someone, or something, that isn't there. |
| 15 | Surrounded by the tools of a trade that isn't the one they claim. |
| 16 | Wears or carries something far too valuable for who they seem to be. |
| 17 | Flinches at something specific — a word, a symbol, a sound. |
| 18 | Won't stop working, eating, or playing even while they talk to you. |
| 19 | Younger or older than the role would lead you to expect. |
| 20 | Beloved by everyone here — or quietly avoided by all of them. |

**And one more thing, if you want it: what do they want *right now*?** *(d8, optional)* — a want turns an NPC from a prop into a player. Skip it for walk-ons; reach for it the moment the heroes start a real conversation.

| d8 | They want… |
|---|---|
| 1 | to be left alone. |
| 2 | to get something off their chest. |
| 3 | to get something *from* the heroes. |
| 4 | to look good in front of someone watching. |
| 5 | to protect someone or something. |
| 6 | to get out of here. |
| 7 | to not get caught. |
| 8 | to finally be taken seriously. |

## Put it together

*The Hidden Hand · repeats your name like an old friend · wants not to get caught* gives you:

> The harbormaster who keeps using your first name, warm as anything — "Anything you need, friend" — and who is, very quietly, the one who already sold you out. Every smile is him making sure you don't notice.

Role, detail, want. That's a character the table will remember and a scene that's secretly ticking.

> **When an NPC starts to matter, promote them.** Most NPCs never need more than a role and a detail. But when one keeps showing up — a recurring ally, the rival who becomes *the* rival — give them a real personality by laying one of the seven **archetypes** over them ([Chapter 16](16-cool-characters.md)). The role says what they do for the story; the archetype says how they *act* while they do it. That's also exactly how you build an antagonist — see Chapter 16.
